# market-sophistication.md — Schwartz's Five Stages + Detection

## The principle

Every market passes through five stages of sophistication. Each stage represents how saturated the market is with competing claims. A claim that works brilliantly at one stage fails completely at another. The researcher's job: locate the market's stage *before* recommending a positioning.

**Default assumption: Stage 3 or 4.** If the user cannot point to evidence that they are genuinely first, assume 3–4.

---

## The five stages

| Stage | Market state | What works | What fails |
|-------|-------------|------------|------------|
| **1 — First to market** | No competing claims exist. The benefit is new. | State the benefit plainly: "You can lose weight." | Any complexity — the market doesn't need convincing yet. |
| **2 — Claims arrive** | Competitors have made the same basic claim. | Enlarge or intensify the claim: "Lose 30 pounds in 30 days." | The plain promise — it's now noise. |
| **3 — Mechanism enters** | Claims saturate; the market stops believing bigger promises. | Introduce a *new mechanism*: "Lose weight with the metabolic reset protocol." | Bigger claims — the market has heard them all and is skeptical. |
| **4 — Mechanism competition** | Every competitor now has a mechanism. | Elaborate and prove the mechanism: more specifics, more evidence, more dimensionalization. | A bare mechanism name — needs proof now. |
| **5 — Identity** | All mechanisms exhausted; the market trusts nothing. | Brand, community, belonging, identity: "We're the kind of person who…" | Any factual claim — the market has stopped believing claims entirely. |

---

## How to detect the stage

Ask: **what does the top of the market currently say?**

Pull 5–10 competitors in the user's space. Read their headlines, their homepage hooks, their ad copy.

- **Everyone leads with a benefit** → Stage 2 (early claims; some competition but not saturated)
- **Everyone has a named method/system/framework** → Stage 3–4
- **Top players barely mention what they do; lead with identity, community, proof** → Stage 4–5
- **No one is saying anything** → Stage 1 (rare; verify before acting on it)

**Red flag:** A user who says "I've never seen anyone do exactly what I do" is often describing Stage 1 — but is frequently wrong. Probe: have they searched broadly, including adjacent markets? Adjacent markets often carry the same offer under a different name.

---

## The strategic instruction for each stage

- **If Stage 1:** State the benefit. Be clear, not clever. The market will come to you.
- **If Stage 2:** Intensify or narrow the claim. Who specifically? How much? How fast?
- **If Stage 3 (most common):** Build and name a unique mechanism. The mechanism is the load-bearing element — everything else supports it.
- **If Stage 4:** The mechanism must be elaborated, proven, and dimensionalized. Add case studies, specific results, visual proof, authority signals.
- **If Stage 5:** The offer almost doesn't matter; the brand and community are the offer. Rare; usually only applies to markets 20+ years deep.

---

## The mechanism's dual job

In Stage 3–4, the mechanism must do two things simultaneously:

1. **Explain why past attempts failed** — "You've tried diets before and they didn't stick because they were designed for day-shift metabolism, not night-shift cortisol patterns."
2. **Explain why this is different** — "The Night-Shift Reset Protocol recalibrates around your actual hormonal cycle, not a generalized one."

A mechanism that only explains what it does is incomplete. A mechanism that explains *why the alternatives don't work and why this one does* is the full version.

---

## Tier these claims

- "My market is in Stage 1" → T3 until verified with a competitor audit
- "There's a gap in the market for X" → T3 until the user can show evidence of demand with no supply
- Stage placement from competitor copy → T2 (observable, but copy changes; note the date)
