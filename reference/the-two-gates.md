# the-two-gates.md — The Mode-Aware Opening Gate

## Why mode matters before anything else

The research is different for each mode. A scratch investigation starts with no data — you're building toward first principles. A sharpen investigation has primary data (what's already selling and what's breaking) — you're diagnosing a live system.

Running a scratch gate on someone who has an existing offer wastes their time. Running a sharpen gate on someone who has nothing yet sets the wrong frame. Ask first.

---

## If mode is already stated, absorb it and skip the mode question.

**If mode is not stated:** Ask one question before anything else:

> *"Are you building an offer from zero, or refining one you already have? That determines where I start."*

One word is enough: "scratch" or "sharpen." Do not begin research until you know.

---

## The Scratch Gate

**Use when:** The user has no existing offer, is pivoting entirely, or is starting a new line of business.

**The investigation is external:** What does the market show? What's the gap? Who has purchasing power and urgency?

Ask the three questions — absorb what's already given, ask only what's missing:

### Scratch Question 1: The market
*"What domain are you in, and what do you broadly do or plan to do?"*

This surfaces: the category, the sophistication stage to check, and the competitive landscape to audit.

**What to listen for:**
- Generic categories ("marketing," "coaching," "consulting") → likely Stage 3–4; the mechanism must carry the positioning
- Niche but unverified categories ("AI agents for restaurants") → T3; check whether competitors already own this
- Adjacent-market plays ("I'm a therapist moving into business coaching") → flag the market-transfer risk

### Scratch Question 2: The avatar + purchasing power
*"Who is your dream client? And — critically — do you have any evidence they routinely pay premium prices for this type of outcome? Purchasing power isn't optional."*

This surfaces: whether the avatar is real, whether they have money, and what desire they're already acting on.

**What to listen for:**
- Named, specific types of buyers ("7-figure e-commerce founders") → T2 if purchasing power is inferred; T1 if user has sold to them before
- Vague categories ("small business owners," "entrepreneurs") → not an avatar; probe further
- Confidence without evidence ("they definitely pay for this") → T3; ask what they're currently spending

### Scratch Question 3: Prior attempts
*"What have you already tried to sell — or what have you seen others in this space try? What happened?"*

This surfaces: the sophistication stage (if the market has already seen these claims), the failure mode (wrong avatar? wrong mechanism? wrong channel?), and whether this is genuinely new ground.

**What to listen for:**
- "I tried this and got no traction" → diagnostic of which gate broke (avatar? mechanism? urgency? channel?)
- "I haven't tried anything yet" → no primary data; the thesis will be more T4-weighted; say so
- "I saw competitor X try this and fail" → useful sophistication signal

## The Sharpen Gate

**Use when:** The user has an existing offer with actual pricing and actual clients.

**The investigation is diagnostic:** What does the live system show? What's breaking and why?

### Sharpen Question 1: The current offer
*"What do you sell right now, and what do you charge?"*

This surfaces: whether the pricing reflects a premium position, whether the offer has a named mechanism, and whether the packaging matches the channel.

**What to listen for:**
- "I charge $X but keep discounting" → pricing isn't the problem; urgency or purchasing power is
- "I charge $X per hour" → time-based pricing; no mechanism; no scalability ceiling yet identified
- "I have a retainer / productized offer" → scalability exists; the break point is likely avatar or channel

### Sharpen Question 2: The break point
*"What specifically is breaking? Pick the one that hurts most: conversion, price objections, churn, fulfillment overwhelm, or referral rate."*

This surfaces: which lever is broken. Each break point has a different root cause.

| Break point | Most likely root cause |
|-------------|----------------------|
| Conversion (getting to yes) | Mechanism not credible; avatar doesn't feel urgency; proof insufficient |
| Price objections | Avatar can't afford it; avatar doesn't believe the value; wrong channel for the price |
| Churn | Wrong avatar (they buy but don't succeed); offer overpromises; mechanism doesn't work |
| Fulfillment overwhelm | Too many avatar types (not niched); no standardized process; wrong cost curve |
| Referral rate | Outcome is good but not remarkable; avatar doesn't know who to refer |

### Sharpen Question 3: The split
*"What do your best clients have in common that your worst clients don't?"*

This is the most important question in the sharpen gate. The user's best/worst split is the only T1 data point in offer research. It almost always reveals the real avatar — and the real offer that should be built.

**What to listen for:**
- Clear pattern (industry, company size, mindset, prior experience) → high-confidence avatar signal; weight heavily
- "My best clients just figure it out" → urgency + resourcefulness; "worst clients want to be carried"
- "My best clients came from referrals" → channel signal; the offer converts best on warm trust
