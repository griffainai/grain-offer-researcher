# offer-thesis-template.md — The Output Shape

## The offer thesis: what it is and what it is not

A thesis is a falsifiable claim. It commits to a verdict, names the evidence it's built on, and specifies what would change the verdict.

An offer thesis is not:
- A list of suggestions for improvement
- A framework for thinking about your offer
- An affirmation that your current direction is right
- A hedge ("it depends on a lot of factors")

An offer thesis IS:
- A specific verdict on what to build, rebuild, or change
- Evidence for why that verdict holds
- Three disconfirming findings that would flip it
- The one question that decides whether the verdict stands

---

## The six-section template

### 1. THE VERDICT
One sentence. A call, not a hedge.

Format options:
- **Build this offer:** "[Avatar] + [mechanism] + [specific outcome] — the evidence supports this direction."
- **Rebuild the positioning:** "The offer isn't the problem — the avatar is. Here's who to rebuild around."
- **Fix the mechanism first:** "The offer is sound but the mechanism is not credible enough to survive cold traffic. Here's what needs to change."
- **Deprioritize this market:** "There's no positioning gap here and the purchasing power is insufficient. Here's an adjacent market that has both."

The verdict should be uncomfortable if the evidence calls for it. A researcher who only validates is not a researcher.

---

### 2. THE POSITIONING GAP
What the market is currently missing that this offer fills — and how you know (with tier labels).

A positioning gap is not:
- "The market is underserved" (T3 unless evidenced)
- "I couldn't find anyone doing exactly this" (T3; you may not have looked hard enough)

A positioning gap IS:
- A specific claim no competitor is making (T2 if from a competitor audit; T1 if backed by sales)
- A specific avatar segment no one has named and owned (T2 if inferred from competitor copy; T1 if from direct buyer interviews)
- A specific mechanism that reframes an existing category (T2–T3 until proven in market)

---

### 3. THE MECHANISM
The named unique mechanism that makes this offer different. Must do both jobs:
- Explain why the buyer's past attempts failed
- Explain why this is different

Format: **[Mechanism name]** — [explanation of why it works differently than the common alternative].

Tier it: If the mechanism is proven from the user's own results → T1. If it's based on sound reasoning but untested → T4 (label it). If it's borrowed from adjacent markets → T3 (label it).

---

### 4. THE AVATAR
Specific person. Must include:
- **Who:** The demographic/psychographic profile, as specific as the evidence supports
- **Purchasing power:** Confirmed (T1) or inferred (T4)? Evidence?
- **Urgency:** What's their forcing function right now?
- **Desire being channeled:** What do they actually want (not what the user wants to sell them)?

If avatar is T4 (inferred with no sales data): say so and tell the user how to validate it.

---

### 5. THE LEDGER

**KNOWN** (T1–T2, sourced):
- [List what you actually know with evidence]

**INFERRED** (T3–T4, labeled):
- [List what you're reasoning toward, with the basis]

**UNKNOWN** (gaps):
- [Name every gap explicitly, with how to close it]

The Unknown list is not an apology. It is the research plan. Each unknown should come with a specific action: "Ask your last three clients this exact question," "Pull competitor pricing from their sales page," "Run a 5-person cold outreach test with this mechanism before building the full offer."

---

### 6. THREE FINDINGS THAT WOULD KILL THIS THESIS
The disconfirming evidence. If the researcher found these, they would change the verdict.

Format: "If [specific finding], I would [change the verdict to X] because [reason]."

Examples:
- "If the user's three best clients all came from personal relationships rather than the mechanism, I would conclude the offer is being carried by trust rather than packaging — and the mechanism needs rebuild before cold-channel acquisition will work."
- "If a competitor already dominates this exact avatar with this exact mechanism and has 3+ years of proof, I would recommend moving to Stage 4 (mechanism elaboration) rather than building a new mechanism."
- "If the avatar's typical annual revenue is under $500K, the purchasing power for a $5,000+ offer may be insufficient — I would recommend either repricing or pivoting to a larger-revenue cohort."

---

### 7. THE NEXT QUESTION
The single highest-leverage thing to validate, and exactly how to validate it.

Format: "The most important unknown right now is [X]. Here's how to close it: [specific action with timeline]."

The next question should be something the user can answer within 1–2 weeks through direct action — not something that requires building the full offer first.

Most common next questions:
- "Does the avatar have purchasing power for this price?" → Run 5 sales conversations with that specific type of buyer at the full price; track objections.
- "Is the mechanism believable?" → Present the mechanism (without the price) to 5 people in the avatar; ask if they believe it and if they've heard it before.
- "Is the positioning gap real?" → Spend 2 hours auditing competitor copy; document every mechanism claim and every avatar they're targeting.
- "What is the best client's profile?" → Interview your three best clients with these four questions: [questions listed].
