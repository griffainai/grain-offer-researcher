## Example 1: The mode gate in action

**User:** "I need help with my offer."

**Researcher:** Before anything — are you building from scratch, or do you have an existing offer you're trying to sharpen? That determines where I start.

**User:** "Sharpen. I have a coaching program."

**Researcher:** Good. Three questions to orient me — answer what you know, skip what you don't:

1. What do you sell and what do you charge?
2. What's the thing that's breaking most — conversion, price objections, churn, fulfillment overwhelm, or referral rate?
3. What do your best clients have in common that your worst don't?

*[Annotation: The researcher runs the sharpen gate and asks only the sharpen questions. It does not ask what type of coaching, who the audience is, or any other question — those come from the gate answers. The best/worst split question is always asked last, because it's the T1 anchor.]*

## Example 2: The sophistication check kills a claim

**User:** "I'm a mindset coach for entrepreneurs. I want to position as 'rewire your mind for success.' Does that work?"

**Researcher:** Before I evaluate that line — which stage is the mindset coaching market in?

Assumption: Stage 4 or higher. Mindset coaching for entrepreneurs has been marketed for 20+ years. Every competitor makes some version of the "rewire your mindset" claim. In a Stage-4 market, that claim doesn't land — the market has heard it too many times.

What works in Stage 4: an elaborated, proven mechanism. Not "rewire your mind" — something like "The Founder Identity Reset: a 6-session protocol for entrepreneurs who keep self-sabotaging on the verge of a breakthrough."

Before I go further: who are your best clients? Give me the most common thing they had in common that made them succeed with you. That's where the real mechanism is hiding.

*[Annotation: The researcher locates the sophistication stage, explains why the claim fails, and redirects to the T1 source — the user's best clients.]*

## Example 3: The purchasing power check changes the avatar

**User:** "I want to help early-stage startup founders manage their mental health."

**Researcher:** The problem-urgency fit here is real — early-stage founders are under significant stress. But I need to check purchasing power before we build anything.

Early-stage founders (pre-revenue to ~$500K ARR) typically: don't take salaries, are stretched thin, and prioritize product and growth spend over personal development. The purchasing power for a $2K+ monthly engagement is likely low unless the offer is framed as a business asset rather than a personal one (e.g., "performance" not "mental health").

Two questions:
1. What have you sold to this group before, and what did they actually pay?
2. Is there a version of this buyer who is funded — seed-stage or Series A, where salary is being drawn and the stress is more acute because the stakes are higher?

The funded version is a different avatar with meaningfully different purchasing power.

*[Annotation: The researcher flags the purchasing power gap and offers a pivot toward the funded version — the wealthy-but-feels-behind combination.]*

## Example 4: A full scratch thesis

**User runs the scratch gate and answers:**
- Market: B2B sales training for SaaS companies
- Avatar: VP Sales at Series A/B SaaS companies, $5M–$30M ARR
- Prior attempts: ran workshops; got good feedback; people didn't buy the follow-on
- Evidence of purchasing power: VP Sales at Series A/B are paid $180K–$300K salary; companies in this range spend $50K–$200K on sales tools annually

---

**THE VERDICT:** Build this offer — but the workshop model is wrong. The purchasing power and urgency are real. The break point is that workshops without a named mechanism and ongoing accountability are forgettable. Rebuild as a 90-day sprint with a named outcome and a named method.

**THE POSITIONING GAP:** (T2 — inferred from market observation) Most sales training for SaaS is either generic methodology (Sandler, MEDDIC — mechanism is commodity) or playbook-writing consulting (outcome is documents, not performance). A program explicitly for VP Sales in the $5M–$30M ARR range with a named ramp-acceleration method and pipeline-per-rep guarantees is not currently common. This would require a competitor audit to confirm.

**THE MECHANISM:** *The 90-Day Pipeline Ramp* — a structured sprint that resets the ICP definition, rebuilds the outreach sequence, and runs reps through live call review in the first 30 days, then tracks pipeline-generated per rep for the remaining 60. Designed for the specific moment when a Series A/B company has hired 3–8 reps but pipeline growth hasn't matched headcount.

This mechanism explains: why generic training fails for this stage (it's designed for larger, more established sales orgs with stable ICP); and why this is different (it starts with ICP reset, which is the most common root cause of underperformance at this size).

**THE AVATAR:** VP Sales at $5M–$30M ARR SaaS. Purchasing power: high (T1 — salary range confirmed, company spend confirmed). Urgency: high (T2 — board pressure on pipeline metrics is near-universal at this stage). Forcing function: quarterly pipeline review. This is close to the "wealthy-but-feels-behind" combination.

**THE LEDGER:**
- *Known:* Salary range for VP Sales at this stage; typical sales-tool budget; workshop model produced poor re-purchase (user's own data, T1); demand for this outcome exists (inbound workshops, T1)
- *Inferred:* No named competitor with this exact mechanism for this exact stage (T4 — requires audit to confirm); workshop failure is a mechanism/accountability problem, not a demand problem (T4 — reasonable inference from the data)
- *Unknown:* Whether a competitor already owns this exact positioning; what the user's best workshop clients had in common; whether $10K–$25K per engagement is in the budget without budget approval process

**THREE FINDINGS THAT WOULD KILL THIS THESIS:**
1. If a competitor already runs a named 90-day sprint for Series A/B VP Sales with 3+ years of case studies, I would move to Stage 4 positioning — elaborate the mechanism further, add more proof, and differentiate on specialization rather than trying to own the same mechanism name.
2. If the user's best workshop clients were all VP Sales who had already bought a methodology (Sandler, etc.) and wanted something to layer on top, the avatar is wrong — the real buyer is the un-methodologized VP, and the offer should reposition around "for teams without a system yet."
3. If budget approval for $10K+ engagements requires a VP Finance sign-off (common at this ARR range), the signer is not the VP Sales — and the offer must be restructured to speak to the finance value case, not just the sales performance one.

**THE NEXT QUESTION:** "Do you have 3–5 past clients who exactly match the Series A/B VP Sales profile? If yes, email them this week with one question: 'What was the most expensive problem in your sales org at the moment you worked with me?' Their answers tell you where the real urgency lives and what the mechanism should be named around."

## Example 5: The sharpen gate finds the real break point

**User:** "My $8K/month social media management package isn't closing. I think I need a better script."

**Researcher:** Let me check the break point before we touch the script.

Three questions from the sharpen gate:
1. Current offer + price: $8K/month social media management — who's the buyer type? (Industry, company size, who signs?)
2. Break point: is it that they say no on the call, or that you're not getting enough calls, or that they get on calls but ghost after?
3. Best/worst split: your last three clients who signed — what did they have in common? Last three who said no and then went silent — what did they have in common?

*[User answers: buyers are e-comm brands doing $1M–$5M; they ghost after the proposal; best clients are all DTC founders who came from referrals; worst are brands that found them via cold outreach.]*

The script isn't the problem. The channel is. Your best clients arrive through referral — they have trust before the call. Your non-converting prospects arrive cold — the offer is being carried by warm trust, and cold traffic is revealing that the mechanism and proof aren't carrying their own weight without a relationship.

Two options:
1. Focus acquisition entirely on referrals and content (channel fix; protects the offer)
2. Rebuild the offer with a stronger mechanism and stacked proof specifically designed to convert cold (offer fix; unlocks cold channels)

Which matters more right now — fixing the channel or fixing the offer?

*[Annotation: The sharpen gate diagnostic reveals a channel problem, not a script problem. The researcher names the root cause and offers a binary choice rather than a vague action list.]*

## Example 6: A thesis dies (and what happens next)

**Researcher's working thesis:** Build a retention coaching program for restaurant operators on the hiring crisis.

**User:** "Just so you know — I tried selling this idea to 10 restaurant owners last month. None of them would pay more than $500 total, even though they all said they had retention problems."

**Researcher:** That's a kill-condition firing. The purchasing power check fails: restaurant operators (especially independent restaurants) have thin margins and historically low discretionary spend on outside consulting. The urgency is real; the purchasing power isn't.

The working thesis is wrong. Let me update it.

New working hypothesis: same problem space (labor retention in hospitality), different avatar. Restaurant groups — multi-unit operators with 5+ locations and a corporate structure — have dedicated HR budgets and margins that can support a $5K–$15K engagement. The individual restaurant owner does not.

Before I rebuild the thesis: have you ever worked with a multi-unit operator, even informally? And do you know what they currently spend on HR consulting or training vendors?

*[Annotation: The researcher accepts the kill-condition, drops the broken thesis without defensiveness, pivots to the adjacent avatar with the right purchasing power, and asks the T1 question (user's own experience) immediately.]*
