# pricing-architecture.md — Premium Flywheel, Category King

## Pricing IS strategy

Price is not a number chosen at the end. It is the engine of the business. It determines what kind of company you can build.

**The premium flywheel:**
Higher price → better margin → better talent → better fulfillment → better outcomes → stronger testimonials and proof → justified higher prices → repeat.

**The discount flywheel (reverse):**
Lower price → thin margins → cheaper talent → worse fulfillment → worse outcomes → weak proof → pressure to lower prices further → repeat.

Every price decision starts one flywheel or the other. Once started, they compound in opposite directions.

## The category-king implication

The most expensive credible option in a niche is almost always the category king — by design, not accident. Premium pricing is both the cause and the signal of that position: they can reinvest more, which produces better outcomes, which justifies higher prices.

**Diagnostic for the researcher:** If the user is not the most expensive credible option in their specific niche, ask why. It is almost always one of:
1. They haven't positioned specifically enough to justify premium (no man's land)
2. They haven't built enough proof to command premium
3. They're comparing their price to the wrong competitors

## Pricing against value, not comparison

The condition for premium pricing: no obvious substitute. If the buyer compares the offer to a competitor and finds a similar-seeming option at lower cost, you're in a price competition. If the offer is specific enough that there's no obvious comparison, you're priced against the *value of the outcome* — which is always higher.

**The test:** What is the outcome worth to the avatar if it works? Price the offer as a small fraction of that value, not as a markup on your time or cost.

## The enterprise bridge

A useful structure for B2B: roughly 90% of clients are SMBs whose volume funds the brand, content, and proof engine — which surfaces the 5–10% of enterprise leads worth multiples more. Enterprise clients have higher margins and longer LTV; SMB volume provides the proof and referral flow that makes enterprise findable.

If the user is purely chasing enterprise with no SMB base, ask: where is the proof coming from? Where is the lead flow? Enterprise sales require a trust infrastructure that usually takes time to build.

## Price points to flag

| Price range | What it usually signals |
|-------------|------------------------|
| Under $500/month or $3K one-time | DWY or info product economics; check that front-end is profitable |
| $2K–$8K/month with full-service | Risk zone: likely no man's land; check avatar specificity and cost curve |
| $10K–$30K/month or $50K+ one-time | High-touch B2B; check LTV:CAC ratio (benchmark: 3:1 minimum) |
| Revenue-share / performance | Signals distrust of outcome probability; usually better to fix the mechanism and charge upfront |
