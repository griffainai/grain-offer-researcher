# positioning-library.md — Barbell, No Man's Land, Category Creation

## The barbell

Viable offer positioning clusters at two extremes:

**High-touch end (B2B / wealthy DFY):**
- Small TAM, high price, high LTV
- Premium DFY work for businesses or wealthy individuals
- Economics are LTV-dependent; high CAC is acceptable
- Margin funds better talent → better outcomes → higher prices (the premium flywheel)

**High-leverage end (B2C / mass DWY):**
- Large TAM, lower price, front-end economics
- Done-with-you: you teach or enable, not deliver
- Must break even on first transaction; cannot rely on long client relationships

## The no man's land (the fatal middle)

The generalist who serves everyone at medium price with high-touch delivery:
- Small TAM economics (few clients, high service cost)
- Low-price ceiling (cannot charge high-end rates without high-end positioning)
- High variable cost (each new client requires near-proportional new effort)
- Margins compress as they scale — not improve

**Signal that someone is in no man's land:**
- Serving 3+ distinct types of clients with no common profile
- Medium pricing ($2K–$8K/month) with full-service delivery
- Revenue growth requires proportional headcount growth
- They describe their work as "it depends" or "we customize for each client"

**The instruction:** If the evidence places the user in no man's land, say so directly. The constraint is positioning, not effort. No amount of marketing will fix it.

## Category creation (rare)

Positioning a new category — not competing in an existing one, but defining a new space — is a Stage-1-to-Stage-3 play. The offer's job is to teach the market what the category is, why the old category is wrong, and why this one is the right frame.

**When it's appropriate:** When the user has a genuinely new mechanism that reframes how the buyer thinks about the problem — not just a better version of an existing solution.

**When it's not:** When the user says "no one else does what I do" but is actually describing an adjacent service that exists under a different name. Check carefully before concluding the user has a category-creation opportunity.

**The Play Bigger finding:** Category kings capture ~76% of the total value of their category. But the path there requires teaching the market a new worldview, not just a better product. The offer is the worldview.

## Category of one

A narrower and more achievable version of category creation: owning a specific combination of avatar + problem + mechanism that no competitor has claimed. The buyer's decision is between this offer and nothing — there's no obvious comparison. This is what the 3S formula produces when executed fully.

**How to check:** Can the user truthfully say "I am the only [role] who does [mechanism] for [avatar]"? If yes and if that combination has buyers with purchasing power, they have a category of one.
