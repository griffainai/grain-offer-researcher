# guarantee-design.md — Guarantee Architecture

## What guarantees actually do

Guarantees serve **marketing differentiation** more than they serve close rates. The primary job is to make the offer stand out and reverse perceived risk enough to earn attention — not to be the thing that closes the deal.

Three counterintuitive truths:

1. **People who buy specifically because of a guarantee tend to be worse clients.** They arrive risk-focused, not outcome-focused. In local markets, guarantee-driven offers fatigue even faster.
2. **Guarantee structure should track ticket size.** Bigger, broader guarantees work with lower-ticket B2C. At high ticket with high fulfillment cost, an unconditional refund means eating both the refund *and* the cost of delivery. Use conditional guarantees at high ticket.
3. **Guarantees can be stacked.** Layering an unconditional 30-day with a conditional 90-day future-paces the buyer into believing the outcome is likely — which is the real psychological work being done.

## The two foundational types

**Unconditional:** "Pay to try it; refund if you don't like it." Maximum perceived risk reversal. Best for lower-ticket, B2C, or entry-level offers where fulfillment cost is low.

**Conditional:** "If you do [specific actions] and don't get [specific result] in [specific timeframe], we do [specific remedy]." Best for high-ticket, B2B, where unconditional refund would destroy economics. The conditions protect against buyers who don't implement.

## Common structures

- **Refund guarantee:** Full or partial refund within a window
- **Performance guarantee:** "We work for free until you hit the result"
- **Cover-the-cost guarantee:** "If it doesn't work, we'll pay your ad spend/time/costs"
- **Extended guarantee:** "If you haven't gotten X in Y months, we extend free until you do"
- **Triple-your-money-back:** Used for bold, high-confidence offers; the boldness is the differentiation signal

## When to recommend a guarantee

- The offer is at a price point where the buyer feels meaningful financial risk
- The mechanism is proven and the user is confident in the result
- The market is at a stage where differentiation through risk reversal is still possible (early-stage guarantee; late-stage markets have heard every guarantee)
- The user can absorb the occasional refund without destroying the unit economics

## When not to recommend a guarantee

- The mechanism is unproven and the user would be guaranteeing outcomes they're not confident in
- The fulfillment cost is high enough that an unconditional refund destroys the deal economics
- The market is already fatigued with guarantees (local markets; mature categories)
- The user wants a guarantee to compensate for a weak mechanism — the fix is the mechanism, not the guarantee
