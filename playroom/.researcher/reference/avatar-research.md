# avatar-research.md — Desire Mechanics, Purchasing Power, Urgency

## The core law

You cannot create desire. You can only channel desire that already exists. The most profitable offers attach to a river of demand that is already flowing and redirect it — they do not try to manufacture want where there is none.

**The implication:** Before recommending any avatar, confirm that the desire is already present and strong, not something the user will have to educate the market into feeling.

---

## The two axes: purchasing power and urgency

Every avatar sits somewhere on two axes. Both matter. An avatar with only one of the two is a limited market.

### Axis 1: Purchasing power
Does this person actually have money to pay premium prices for this outcome?

**Evidence that confirms it:**
- What do they currently spend on similar outcomes? (T1 if you can observe it; T4 if you're inferring)
- What do competitors charge them, and do they pay? (T2 if from observable market; T3 if estimated)
- What is their income / business revenue? (T1 if stated; T3 if estimated from segment)

**The trap:** "SMBs" is not a purchasing-power check. SMBs range from a one-person Etsy shop to a $10M construction company. The question is whether *this specific type* of SMB routinely pays for *this type of outcome*.

### Axis 2: Urgency
Does this person feel acute pain or strong desire right now — or is this a "someday I should" problem?

**Evidence that confirms it:**
- Are they actively searching for solutions? (Keyword volume, forum activity, DM inquiries — T2)
- Have they tried and failed already? (Past purchase evidence — T1 if observable)
- Is there a forcing function? (Deadline, external event, compliance requirement — T1)

**The trap:** "Everyone needs better marketing" has zero urgency. It's a chronic condition, not an acute one. Urgency requires a reason to act *now*.

## The three avatar positions

### 1. Below-average to average (acute pain)
The person is in distress — broke, stuck, failing, fired. High urgency, large market, low purchasing power. They will act immediately but cannot pay much.

- Best for: B2C, front-end economics, high volume, lower ticket
- Danger: Churn is high; clients who buy from desperation may not execute

### 2. Average to excellent (aspiration)
The person is already doing okay and wants to level up. Low urgency, small market, high purchasing power. They can pay enormous sums but are in no hurry and are rare.

- Best for: B2B, premium coaching, high-touch DFY
- Danger: Long sales cycles; hard to manufacture urgency

### 3. The nuclear combination
**The wealthy buyer who feels behind.** High purchasing power + high urgency in the same person. Rare and extraordinarily valuable.

Examples:
- A successful business owner who watched competitors lap them with AI and feels panicked
- A high-net-worth person going through a divorce (assets to protect + terror of losing them)
- A funded startup that just missed a key metric and needs to fix acquisition before the board meeting

When the user's avatar has this combination, name it explicitly and protect it. This is the rarest and most profitable offer environment that exists.

## The desire-channeling test

The user's offer should attach to desire the buyer already has — not desire the user wishes the buyer had.

**Test:** Complete this sentence from the avatar's point of view:
*"I desperately want _____ because _____. I'm terrified of _____ and it keeps me up at night."*

If the user cannot complete this sentence with confidence, they don't know their avatar well enough yet. If the blanks fill in with "I guess they want to grow their business" — that's a T4 inference at best.

**The pivot move:** The offer that channels existing desire is almost always different from the capability being sold.

| What the user sells | What the avatar desires | The channeling pivot |
|--------------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| Speaker coaching | More high-ticket clients | "Get on stages that fill your pipeline" |
| AI automation | Less time on admin | "Recover the $X/month you're losing to manual work" |
| Bookkeeping | Peace of mind | "Never get surprised by a tax bill again" |
| Executive coaching | Status, promotion | "Make the C-suite in 18 months" |

The capability is identical. The desire being channeled is completely different. One is a trickle; the other is a flood.

## Source discipline on avatar claims

- "My target customer is X" → T4 (inferred) until validated with sales data or direct interviews
- "They typically pay Y for this" → T3 if estimated; T1 if from actual invoices or public pricing
- "This niche is underserved" → T3 always — demand for evidence before acting on it
- Best/worst client split from the user's own data → T1 (primary data; weight heavily)
